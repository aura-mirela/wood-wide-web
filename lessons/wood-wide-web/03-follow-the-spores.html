Lesson 03 — Follow the spores

Episode 3: Follow the spores. The mushroom releases a cloud of spores that drift through the forest; the camera leaves the user's hands and glides after them along a smooth path.

Concepts — spores: particles or instances?

Decision rule for this whole project:

  • Small spores (hundreds–thousands, dots of light): THREE.Points. One draw call, one vertex per spore, all motion computed in the vertex shader from per-point attributes + uTime. CPU does nothing per frame. They cannot have real shape — each is a camera-facing square you shade into a soft disc.
  • Big spores (a handful, with geometry, lesson 06): InstancedMesh. One draw call, real 3D geometry per instance, per-instance matrix + custom attributes. Use when a spore must be an object (clickable, lit, textured), not a glow.
  • Rule of thumb: if it's a light, it's a point; if it's a thing, it's an instance.

GPU-driven particle motion: don't move particles in JS. Give each point attributes at creation (aSeed, aOffset, aSize) and compute position in the vertex shader as a pure function of time: pos = emitter + rise(t) + curlNoise(seed, t). Stateless = free rewind/scrub.

Concepts — curves

  • THREE.CubicBezierCurve3 — 4 control points, exactly like SVG/CSS easing curves but in 3D. curve.getPoint(t) for position, curve.getTangent(t) for direction, t ∈ [0,1].
  • THREE.CatmullRomCurve3 — give it waypoints, it passes through them. Usually nicer for camera paths: you author "stations", it handles smoothness.
  • Arc length matters: getPoint(0.5) is NOT the halfway point by distance (control points bunch speed up). getPointAt(t) is the arc-length-parameterized version — use it for anything the eye will notice, i.e. the camera.
  • Camera along a curve: each frame set camera.position = curve.getPointAt(p) and camera.lookAt(target), where target is its own smoothed point (the spore cloud centroid, or a second slower curve). Never hard-attach lookAt to something jittery.
  • Who owns the camera? OrbitControls and your curve flight will fight. Introduce a camera-mode state: free | flying | focused. Disable controls (enabled={false}) while flying. This state machine grows into the episode system in lesson 07.

Build steps

  1. Spore burst: BufferGeometry with ~2000 points at the gill ring (random angle, radius ≈ cap radius, y ≈ gill height). Attributes aSeed, aSize.
  2. Vertex shader: time-based rise + horizontal noise drift + slow spiral. A uProgress uniform (0→1) drives the whole release so you can trigger it on click, pause it, or scrub it.
  3. Fragment shader: soft disc via gl_PointCoord, warm glow color, AdditiveBlending, depthWrite: false. Fade each spore in at birth and out at the end of its arc (alpha from the same time function).
  4. Camera path: author 4–6 waypoints in a CatmullRomCurve3 from the mushroom clearing, through the trees, ending looking down at the future mycelium ground. Debug-draw the curve as a line while authoring (drei <Line>), with small spheres at waypoints.
  5. Flight: on "release" click → camera mode flying, advance p with an ease-in-out (p = smoothstep over a duration, or lerp toward 1). LookAt a point that leads the spore cloud slightly.
  6. Scrub option (storyboard says "drag to follow"): map pointer drag or wheel delta to p directly instead of time. Same curve, user-driven. Clamp and smooth (p chases targetP).
  7. Hand control back: when p ≥ 1, camera mode → focused on the ground, re-enable (or restrict) controls. Broken camera handoffs are the #1 polish killer — make this seamless before moving on.

Exercises

  • Plot getPoint vs getPointAt sample points as spheres on one curve and see the arc-length difference.
  • Give the camera roll: tilt camera.up slightly by the curve's curvature for a banked, organic feel. Subtle.
  • Make 2% of spores brighter "hero" spores whose size pulses — guides where the eye should go during the flight.

Checkpoint

  • Why does stateless (pure-function-of-time) particle motion make scrubbing free?
  • When points bunch in a bezier, what happens to getPoint(t) spacing and why?
  • Why must the spore material not write depth, and what artifact appears if it does?

Quiz

1. 2,000 drifting glowing spores. Best representation:

They're lights, not things: one vertex each, GPU-driven, one draw call. (b) works but pays for geometry nobody can see.

2. "Stateless" particle motion means:

No accumulated state; that's what makes scrubbing and rewinding free.

3. curve.getPoint(0.5) vs curve.getPointAt(0.5):

Control-point spacing distorts t; the eye notices speed changes, so cameras use getPointAt.

4. The camera flight stutters every time the spore cloud bounces. Root cause:

Never hard-attach lookAt to noisy data; smooth it or lead it.

5. During the camera flight, OrbitControls must be:

Two systems writing the camera fight; a single owner at a time, managed by explicit state.
