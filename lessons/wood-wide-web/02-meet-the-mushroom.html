Lesson 02 — Meet the mushroom

Episode 2: Click the mushroom. Tap the Fly Agaric to reveal its identity — a card with its classification, and the mushroom answers with light.

Concepts

  • LatheGeometry = pottery. You define a 2D profile (array of Vector2, x = radius, y = height) and three.js spins it around the Y axis. One profile curve gives you stem + cap in a single surface. Gills can be a second, inner lathe with a wavy radius.
  • The red cap with white spots, procedurally. In the fragment shader: base red, then white spots where a cellular/worley-ish pattern crosses a threshold (or: dot the surface with N random points passed as a uniform array and whiten within a radius — simpler, fine for ~20 spots).
  • Fresnel rim light is the single highest-value trick in this whole project: pow(1.0 - dot(viewDir, normal), p) makes edges glow. It is the "this thing is magical/alive" signal, and later the hover affordance.
  • Raycasting is free in R3F. onClick / onPointerOver on a mesh just work. Understand what happens underneath: a ray from the camera through the pointer, intersected against the mesh's triangles.
  • UX of discoverability. The user must suspect the mushroom is clickable: idle pulse (subtle emissive breathing), hover response (fresnel brightens, cursor becomes pointer), click response (flash + panel). Three tiers, each stronger than the last.
  • Info panel. Two options — drei's <Html> anchors DOM to a 3D point (panel follows the mushroom), or a fixed overlay (position: fixed, like the storyboard's card). The card itself is pure CSS: backdrop-filter: blur(), translucent dark background, 1px light border, slide+fade entrance via a @keyframes or transition on mount.

Build steps

  1. Sketch the profile on paper first: stem radius ~0.3, bulge at base, flare into a cap. Turn it into Vector2[] → <latheGeometry args={[points, 48]} />. Iterate with OrbitControls until the silhouette is right.
  2. Material: start meshStandardMaterial red to check the shape, then swap to your shader: red base + white spots + fresnel rim + warm subsurface-ish gradient near the gills (fake it: brighten where normal.y < 0).
  3. A warm pointLight inside the cap sells "glowing from within".
  4. Interaction states as React state: idle | hover | active. Drive a single uGlow uniform (0 / 0.4 / 1) and lerp it every frame toward the target (uGlow += (target - uGlow) * delta * speed) so transitions feel organic. This lerp-toward-target pattern will drive every transition in the project.
  5. The card: species name, Latin name, one-line role in the ecosystem (storyboard: "Kingdom: Fungi … A psychoactive mushroom known for its distinctive red cap…"). Close button. useMediaQuery from react-responsive to position it bottom-sheet-style on mobile vs floating card on desktop.
  6. Dim the rest of the scene while the card is open (a uDim uniform on the ground/trees, or a translucent DOM scrim) — focus is a lighting decision.

Exercises

  • Add onPointerOver/out cursor handling globally (a tiny hook that sets document.body.style.cursor). You'll reuse it for every clickable thing.
  • Animate the spots: make them shimmer slightly with noise + uTime. Taste check: if you notice it immediately, it's too strong.
  • Keyboard accessibility: Enter/Space opens the panel when the mushroom is "focused". Think about what focus even means for a 3D object.

Checkpoint

  • Why does the fresnel term use the view direction, and in which space must the normal and view vector live for the dot product to be honest?
  • Why lerp uniforms in useFrame instead of animating React state?
  • When would you pick <Html> over a fixed overlay, and what does <Html> cost at render time?

Quiz

1. LatheGeometry builds a mushroom from:

x is radius, y is height; one curve gives stem and cap.

2. The fresnel term pow(1.0 - dot(viewDir, normal), p) is strongest:

The dot product goes to 0 at grazing angles, so 1 - dot peaks there; that's the rim.

3. Hover state changes uGlow from 0 to 0.4. The storyboard feel requires the change to be gradual. Best pattern:

Frame-rate-independent-ish, organic, no React churn; make it exact with 1 - exp(-k * delta) damping.

4. Why can onClick on a mesh be expensive if the mesh has very many triangles?

Intersection is per-triangle (bounds help, but dense meshes still cost); a simple invisible collider proxy is a common fix.

5. drei's <Html> vs a fixed DOM overlay — a real tradeoff of <Html> is:

Per-frame style updates for tracking; fine for one card, costly for dozens.
