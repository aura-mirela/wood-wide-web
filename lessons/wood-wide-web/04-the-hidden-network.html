Lesson 04 — The hidden network

Episode 4: Discover the ground. The camera is low. As the cursor moves over the forest floor, the mycelium wakes up beneath it — a glowing web revealed patch by patch.

Concepts

  • Grow, don't draw. The network must come from an algorithm so it looks found, not authored. Two good algorithms, in order of difficulty:
    1. Branching random walk (start here). Tips walk outward from the mushroom base in small steps; each step adds noise-based turning; each tip has a chance to split; tips die at max length or map edge. Store every segment.
    2. Space colonization (upgrade later). Scatter "nutrient" attraction points; each growth step, every tip moves toward the average of nearby attractors; consumed attractors are removed. Produces the uncanny searching look of real hyphae — and it's the same algorithm you'd use for lightning or roots.
  • Polyline → renderable geometry. Options:
    • TubeGeometry per branch (a CatmullRomCurve3 through its points) — real 3D, merge them into one BufferGeometry or draw calls will kill you (BufferGeometryUtils.mergeGeometries).
    • Fat lines / ribbons for distant filaments — cheaper.
    • A good scene mixes: tubes near camera, lines far away.
  • The attribute trick that makes everything later possible: while growing, record for every vertex aDistance = distance from the network's root along the growth path (graph distance, not straight-line). Bake it as a buffer attribute. Pulses, reveals, and growth animations all become one-line shader math against aDistance.
  • Cursor reveal. Raycast the pointer against the ground plane each frame → world point → uCursor uniform. Fragment shader: reveal = smoothstep(uRadius, uRadius * 0.4, distance(worldPos, uCursor)), multiply into emissive. Add fbm to the radius edge so the reveal boundary is organic, and lerp uRadius up from 0 so it blooms rather than pops.
  • Persistence (UX choice). Storyboard says the network "wakes up" — explored areas should stay faintly lit. Cheap version: accumulate visited cursor points into a small render-target texture ("fog of war" style) sampled by the shader. Simple version first: just a trail of the last N cursor positions as uniforms.

Build steps

  1. Write the growth algorithm as a pure function: growNetwork(seed, params) → { branches: Vector3[][], nodes: Vector3[] }. No three.js inside; test it by rendering branch polylines with drei <Line> first. Tune: step size, turn angle, split probability, 2.5D flattening (y stays near 0, slight dips).
  2. Identify nodes (split points + tips) — these become clickable in lesson 05 and are where the network connects to tree roots.
  3. Build merged tube geometry. While building, write aDistance per vertex. Verify by coloring the network with aDistance as a gradient — you should see rainbow rings radiating from the root. This debug view is your friend.
  4. Shader: dark base, hot emissive core color (ember orange like the inspiration, or green like real foxfire — your call), slight pulse from sin(aDistance * k - uTime) so energy always seems to flow outward.
  5. Cursor reveal uniform chain: pointer → raycast ground → uCursor, uRadius. Camera in this episode tilts down when the pointer nears the bottom of the screen (storyboard note) — lerp camera pitch from normalized pointer y.
  6. Soil above the web: make the ground from lesson 01 slightly translucent-dark in this mode (uDim), so the network reads as underneath, not painted on.

Exercises

  • Expose growth params (split chance, wander, steps) in leva or simple sliders and regrow on change. Ten minutes of play here teaches more than any article.
  • Grow the network over time instead of instantly: reveal by aDistance < uGrowth with a soft edge. You just made a growth animation with zero per-frame CPU work — this is lesson 07's spore-to-mycelium scene already.
  • Try space colonization and compare silhouettes with your random walk.

Checkpoint

  • Why must branch distance be measured along the graph rather than straight-line from the root? What visual bug appears if you cheat?
  • Why merge tube geometries, and what do you lose by merging (hint: per-branch visibility / raycasting granularity)?
  • Where does the cursor→world raycast happen, and why against the ground plane rather than the tube meshes?

Quiz

1. Space colonization grows toward:

Tips steer toward unconsumed attractors; the consumption is what creates space-filling, searching growth.

2. You render 400 separate TubeGeometry meshes and the frame rate dies. The fix:

Each mesh is a draw call plus state changes; merging collapses them to one.

3. aDistance must be graph distance (along branches), not straight-line from the root, because otherwise:

Energy flows along hyphae; straight-line distance ignores topology, so the animation would ignore the network's shape.

4. The cursor reveal needs the pointer's position on the ground. You get it by:

The standard R3F pattern; against the plane because it's one cheap intersection and gives a stable point even between tubes.

5. The reveal radius edge looks like a perfect circle — too mechanical. Cheapest organic fix:

Noise on the threshold makes the boundary breathe; it's one shader line.
