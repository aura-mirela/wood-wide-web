The episodes (from the storyboard)

  1. Enter the forest — ambient night scene, fog, moon, fireflies
  2. Click the mushroom — raycast, glow, info panel (Fly Agaric card)
  3. Follow the spores — particle release, camera follows a bezier path
  4. Discover the ground — camera tilts down, mycelium network revealed under cursor
  5. Connect the trees — click a node, a signal pulse travels the network
  6. Watch the exchange — nutrients flow tree→fungus and fungus→tree
  7. Click a spore — zoom into the microscopic world (instanced spores)
  8. The cycle continues — spore → mycelium → primordium → mushroom
  9. Reflection — pull back, the forest breathes as one

How to work through a lesson

  1. Read the lesson's Concepts section before touching code.
  2. Build it yourself from the Build steps. They are directions, not code dumps. When stuck for more than ~30 min on one step, ask for a hint on that step only.
  3. Do the Exercises — they are where the learning actually happens.
  4. Answer the Checkpoint questions out loud or in writing. If you can't, reread before moving on.
  5. Take the Quiz — pick answers, hit "Check answers". Miss two or more → redo the related exercise.
  6. Tick the lesson's "Done when" boxes and the lesson box below. They are saved in your browser.

Ground rules for this project

  • No imported 3D models. Everything is generated: lathe profiles, tubes along curves, instanced primitives, noise-displaced planes.
  • GLSL first, TSL later. Shaders live in src/shaders/*.glsl and are imported as strings (vite-plugin-glsl is configured in vite.config.ts, and src/glsl.d.ts teaches TypeScript about those imports).
  • One scene, many states. The whole experience is a single R3F <Canvas>; episodes change camera, uniforms, and visibility — they don't remount the world.

Reference shelf

  • The Book of Shaders — GLSL fundamentals, read ch. 1–12 alongside lessons 01–04
  • R3F docs and drei docs
  • three.js fundamentals — especially "Custom BufferGeometry" and "Instancing"
