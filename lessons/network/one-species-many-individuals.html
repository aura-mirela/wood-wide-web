1. Individuals are patches, built once

Build the list when the placements exist. A click only reads it. Each individual is one patch of soil: mushrooms that already sit near each other, plus the firs whose bases fall in that same patch.

assignFungalIndividuals(mushroomPlacements, treePlacements, patchRadius):
  individuals = []

  for each mushroom index:
    patch = the individual whose patch this mushroom falls inside
    if patch is missing:
      start a new individual at this mushroom
    else:
      add this mushroom to that individual

  for each individual:
    individual.treeIndices = firs whose base is inside the patch
    individual.strands = nearest-neighbor links
      using only this individual's mushrooms
    individual.rootLinks = one link from the nearest of those mushrooms
      to each of its firs

  return individuals

Distance still decides which mushrooms share a patch, and which pairs become a strand. It no longer decides which network a click may enter. A mushroom near the edge of patch A is a member of A. It does not grow a strand into B.

2. A click resolves through membership

on mushroom click(index):
  individual = the individual whose mushroomIndices contain index
  revealedId = individual.id

draw:
  strands of revealedId
  root flares of revealedId.treeIndices
  leave every other individual dark

Walk the example. Mushrooms 1, 2, and 3 belong to A, with firs A and B. Mushrooms 4 and 5 belong to B, with fir C.

  • Click mushroom 2. Reveal mycelium A and the exchange on firs A and B.
  • Mushrooms 4 and 5 stay dark, even when one of them is the nearest mushroom in the scene.
  • Click mushroom 4 later. Reveal mycelium B and fir C on their own.

buildNeighborStar does the other thing today. It links the clicked index to nearby indexes and will join A to B when mushroom 3 sits close to mushroom 4. The new click handler never calls it with the whole placement list.

3. The root scan under the revealed firs

public/models/fir_trunk._spruce_trunk.glb is a photogrammetry scan, Sketchfab title “Fir trunk. Spruce trunk,” license CC-BY-NC-SA. The fir model is CC-BY. Five meshes in the file are chunks of that one scan, split near the 65,000-vertex limit. Together they are about 300,000 vertices. The bounds match, so you draw all five, and you draw them only for the firs of the revealed individual. Instancing the scan under every desktop fir is about 80 copies of that vertex load.

In scene space the fir stands from y = 0 to about 4.4, and the scan hangs from about y = -0.09 down to y = -2.17. It is about 2.3 units wide, which is as wide as the whole fir, and its center sits about 0.37 off the origin on X. Look at the scan once before you commit: the title names a fir trunk and a spruce trunk, so both stumps may be in the mesh.

RootFlares(treePlacements, revealedTreeIndices):
  load the trunk scan
  for each mesh chunk in the scan:
    instance it

  for each placement index in revealedTreeIndices:
    use that fir's x, elevation, rotationY
    use a rootScale smaller than placement.scale
    shift X so the flare center sits on the trunk axis
    shift Y so the collar meets the soil
    leave the rest of the scan below the terrain

  skip every fir that is not in the revealed individual

Same placement list, not a second scatter. A second seed would put flares under empty ground. Terrain relief is only about 0.35, so a sunk scan stays buried except for the collar you chose to leave at the surface. Mycelium rootLinks end at that collar, not at the center of the trunk.

4. Lines for hyphae, points for spores

A strand inside one individual is a continuous hypha. drei’s <Line> is that stroke. Give it the Bézier samples you already know how to build, one line per strand, with worldUnits so the thickness stays a hair in the world when the camera comes closer. A revealed individual has a handful of strands, so one line each is enough. Pixel width would stay a constant screen stroke.

MyceliumLines(individual):
  for each strand in individual.strands and individual.rootLinks:
    samples = points along that strand, on the terrain, slightly above it
    draw Line(points = samples, worldUnits, a hair width)

SporeCloud, later, separate component:
  each point stores a birthplace on a cap, a direction, and a lifetime
  the vertex shader moves it from uTime
  the fragment shader is the soft disc you already have

The orange and cyan arrows on the bark are a later ribbon on the revealed firs. <Line> does not taper, soften, or form those arrowheads. The soil web and the trunk exchange stay two drawings.

5. The overlay for this beat

StoryOverlay is position: fixed over the page, with pointer-events: none, so a click still hits a mushroom. It renders two placeholders today:

StoryOverlay:
  StoryChapter
  Action

StoryChapter is the heading and the paragraph. Action is the gesture: a round icon and one sentence, and only when the paragraph does not already say that gesture. The two science cards on the exchange panel are neither. They are a new sibling, ExchangeLegend, shown only after a click has revealed an individual.

StoryOverlay:
  StoryChapter          // heading + paragraph, from storyText.js
  ExchangeLegend        // only when an individual is revealed
  Action                // only when this chapter has a gesture

before any individual is revealed:
  title: Meet one individual
  body: Every mushroom here is a fly agaric.
        A click reveals one underground individual,
        not every mushroom nearby.
  action: Click a mushroom.
  legend: hidden

after mushroom 2 reveals individual A:
  title: See the exchange
  body: This fly agaric is one individual.
        Its mycelium reaches these firs.
        Sugars move down from the tree.
        Water and minerals move up from the fungus.
  action: Watch the flow.
  legend:
    Tree → Fungi    sugars from photosynthesis
    Fungi → Tree    water and minerals from the soil

The legend copy is the two cards in public/inspiration/storyboard.png. It stays out of Action and out of StoryChapter. storyText.js keeps the words. StoryChapter and Action stay the components they already are. ExchangeLegend reads the same “an individual is revealed” flag the 3D scene uses to show flares and lines.

Build steps

  1. Write assignFungalIndividuals and log one forest: each mushroom index in exactly one individual, each revealed fir listed on that individual.
  2. Change the click path so the index is looked up in that list. Draw only that individual’s strands.
  3. Replace the point web with one <Line worldUnits> per strand, including the links that end on the root collars.
  4. Instance the trunk scan for revealedTreeIndices only. Tune rootScale and the X/Y shift until the collar meets the fir and the plate is wider than the trunk, not as wide as the crown.
  5. Add the two chapters to storyText.js. Teach StoryChapter and Action to read the active chapter. Mount ExchangeLegend inside StoryOverlay for the revealed state.
  6. Leave spores and the bark ribbon for their own passes. The point shader waits for the spore cloud.

Exercises

  4. Point at StoryOverlay.jsx and name the child that does not exist yet. Say which storyboard cards it owns.
