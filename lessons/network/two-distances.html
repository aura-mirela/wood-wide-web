Network track · why a click listed every fir, then no firs at all

Two distances, one click

What this lesson explains: the click log and the fir scale that are in the scene now, and why one join distance could not both keep individuals small and still reach a trunk. The code is already written. This page is how to read it.

Goal

After this lesson you should be able to:

  • read one console line and say which mushrooms, which firs, and which root flares that click revealed;
  • say why firs [] have roots [] is a stored empty list, and why the same line once printed almost every fir index;
  • name the two desktop distances and which loop uses each one;
  • say why the grown firs are 1.2 on x, y, and z, and why a trunk with no square plate can still be in the log.

1. One distance did both jobs, and neither number worked

Caps and firs are scattered from two masks. Caps sit near other caps. A trunk is often farther from the nearest cap than two caps are from each other. The ground distance is hypot(a.x - b.x, a.y - b.y). Placement y is the ground axis. The scene draws it as world z = -y.

While both tests used the same number, the desktop forest had two bad settings:

Desktop distanceWhat one click did
0.5Twenty-five clicks logged firs [] have roots []. The caps formed small individuals. Almost no trunk was inside 0.5 of any of them, so nothing was stored and nothing grew.
2One click logged firs 0–79 except 34 and 64. Every mushroom in that chain stopped breathing, and every listed fir grew. A walk of gaps no larger than 2 crossed the whole forest, so those caps were one individual, and almost every trunk was within 2 of some cap in it.

An empty JavaScript array becomes a blank inside a template string. `firs ${[]}` prints firs , which looks like the indexes were dropped. JSON.stringify prints [], so an empty list and a missing list are different.

2. Caps stay close. Firs may stand farther

MYCELIUM_MAX_DISTANCE_BY_TIER is the gap that still joins two mushrooms. FIR_MAX_DISTANCE_BY_TIER is the gap that still joins a fir to its nearest mushroom. On the smaller tiers the two tables match. Only desktop splits them: caps at 1, firs at 1.5.

TierMushroom gapFir gap
xs2.22.2
sm1.91.9
md1.71.7

Terrain reads both from the breakpoint flags and passes them into assignFungalIndividuals. The mushroom walk still uses maxDistance. The fir loop uses firMaxDistance, which defaults to maxDistance when the caller does not pass it.

collect mushroom groups with maxDistance

for each fir:
  nearest cap = the closest mushroom whose ground gap is <= firMaxDistance
  if there is no such cap:
    leave this fir out of every individual
  else:
    push this fir index onto that cap's individual
      treeIndices and rootIndices
    push one strand, mushroom → root

A fir is stored once, on the individual of its nearest cap inside the fir gap. Clicking a farther cap does not list that fir. treeIndices and rootIndices receive the same index in the same loop, then both are sorted. They are two names for one list: the tree, and the flare under it.

3. The click prints the record. It does not search again

revealIndividual finds the individual whose mushroomIndices contain the clicked index. If that lookup fails, it returns and prints nothing. If it succeeds, it prints the three lists already on that record, then appends the id when it is not already revealed.

firs [72] have roots [72] from mushrooms [4,11]
firs [] have roots [] from mushrooms [18]

The first line is an individual with caps 4 and 11 and fir 72. The second line is a different individual. Cap 18 was found. No fir was stored on it, because none is within the fir gap of its caps. The brackets are the proof that the list was read. A blank between the words was the old empty-array print.

The same id drives the three visible changes. restingMushroomIndices is the mushroom indexes of every revealed individual, so those caps hold still. rootIndices is the fir indexes, passed both to ScatteredRootFlares and to ScatteredFirTrees as rootedFirIndices. A second click appends the next id. The first individual’s caps stay still, and its firs stay large.

4. The grown fir is the debug. The plate under the soil is a different question

ScatteredFirTrees multiplies placement.scale by 1.2 when the instance index is in rootedFirIndices, and writes that number on x, y, and z. Every other fir keeps placement.scale on all three axes. The layout effect depends on rootedFirIndices, so the matrices update when a click adds indexes.

The root scan is a separate mesh. Its wide plate hangs below the collar, and ROOT_LIFT is only 0.03. From the horizon lock, a fir that is in the log can still show no square on the soil, because the terrain triangle covers the collar. The scale is how you see that the index was stored. The plate is how you see that the scan cleared the ground.

Quiz

1. firs [] have roots [] means:

The lookup succeeded, which is why a line printed. Both stored lists are empty. [] is that empty list made visible.

2. At desktop distance 2, one click grew almost every fir because:

Membership is a walk. A chain of gaps no larger than the mushroom distance is one individual. The firs within the fir distance of that chain were stored on it.

3. On desktop, the two numbers are:

MYCELIUM_MAX_DISTANCE_BY_TIER.desktop is 1. FIR_MAX_DISTANCE_BY_TIER.desktop is 1.5. The smaller tiers use one number for both.

4. A fir grows by 1.2 when:

The 1.2 is applied on x, y, and z inside ScatteredFirTrees. The plate under the soil is the scan, not the scale.
