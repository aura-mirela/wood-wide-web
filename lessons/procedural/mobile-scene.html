Procedural track · breakpoints, viewport, camera, scene cost, lights

What to change for phones and tablets

What this lesson explains: the scene in Experience.jsx is one desktop shot at every width. src/breakpoints/ already names the widths, and nothing reads them. This lesson is the list of changes to make, including the HDR sky and the directional light — and which of those two you leave alone.

Goal

After this lesson you should be able to:

  • say which breakpoint a landscape phone falls into, and why isXs || isSm misses it;
  • pick camera, terrain, instance counts, and pixel ratio from those tiers;
  • size the page with 100dvh on desktop and on a phone;
  • keep the directional light, keep the HDR as the sky and the image-based light, and name the one light change that is actually about cost.

1. Read the bands before you invent a “mobile” flag

FlagWidthTypical device
isXs≤ 575Phone, portrait
isSm576–767Large phone, portrait
isMd768–991Tablet, and a phone turned sideways
isLg992–1199Small laptop
isXl1200–1399Desktop
isXxl≥ 1400Wide desktop

Only one flag is true at a time. The boundaries meet: 575 is xs, 576 is sm, 767 is sm, 768 is md.

A phone in landscape is often about 800–900px wide. That is isMd, not isSm. If “mobile” means isXs || isSm, a sideways phone keeps the desktop terrain.

Use three tiers:

  • Phone — isXs || isSm (≤ 767).
  • Compact — phone or isMd (≤ 991). Landscape phones and tablets live here.
  • Desktop — isLg || isXl || isXxl (≥ 992). This tier keeps today’s scene.

Width does not tell you the frame is tall. Three.js fov is the vertical angle. On a portrait phone the lens still covers 50° up and down, and the sides of the forest get cropped. Add a second query, useMediaQuery({ orientation: 'portrait' }), and use it only for the camera.

2. Do not build the heavy scene twice

useMediaQuery from react-responsive is false on the first render, then flips after mount. On a phone that means Terrain allocates a 256-segment plane, 80 trees, and 160 mushrooms, throws them away, and builds the phone scene. Seed the first value from window.matchMedia so the first render is already the right tier.

Call the hook in Experience. Pass segments, treeCount, and mushroomCount into Terrain. Pass dpr, camera position, and fov to <Canvas>. Pass orbit distances to <OrbitControls>.

3. The page height, on every screen

Use 100dvh on html, body, #root, and the canvas. Do this for desktop too. On a desktop browser 100vh and 100dvh are the same height. On a phone 100vh includes the strip behind the address bar, so the bottom of the scene sits under the browser chrome.

Set the whole chain, not only the canvas. html and body are height: 100% today, and that percentage follows the large viewport. A canvas at 100dvh inside a parent at 100% can be a different height from #root.

Put touch-action: none on the canvas so a drag orbits the forest instead of scrolling the page. index.html already has a viewport meta tag.

4. Reframe the shot when the screen is tall

The desktop shot is position [0, 2, 12], fov: 50, target [-2, 1, -3.5], distance 12–16, polar angle locked at 1.51. Keep the target and the locked polar angle. On a portrait phone, pull the eye back and open the vertical lens so the same forest still fits.

Use the portrait column when isPortrait is true even if the width is sm. A landscape phone (md, not portrait) can stay on the md row. Recompute azimuth only if you move X or the target. These rows change Z and distance along the same aim, so the azimuth you measured in the landscape-camera lesson still applies.

5. Spend fewer triangles where the screen is small

TERRAIN_SEGMENTS = 256 is a 257×257 grid. Each vertex runs five octaves of noise. Phones do not need that density at a pulled-back camera.

xssmmdlg and up
terrain segments6496128256
trees24405680
mushrooms4080110160
canvas dpr[1, 1.25][1, 1.5][1, 1.75][1, 2]

Phone pixel ratios are often 3. An uncapped canvas shades several times as many fragments as the desktop one, on a smaller GPU. The dpr cap is the per-frame win. The segment and instance cuts are the memory and vertex win.

On the compact tier, stop passing bumpMap in TerrainPBRMaterial. You still upload the five 1K maps; you stop sampling the height map in the lighting shader. Desktop keeps bumpScale={0.06}.

6. The HDR and the directional light

There are two lights. They do not cost the same thing, so they do not get the same mobile treatment.

Keep the directional light on every breakpoint

In Experience.jsx it is color #ff4f25, intensity 0.07 * 0.2 (0.014), position [0.56, 0.02, -0.82]. That is a dim orange graze near the horizon, not a sun. One directional light without a shadow map is a few extra multiplies inside MeshStandardMaterial. Removing it on a phone does not change the frame time you can feel, and the forest gets colder than the desktop shot. Copy the same color, intensity, and position into every tier.

Keep the HDR as both the sky and the light

SceneEnvironment loads rosendal_park_sunset_puresky_1k.hdr and sets background, so drei assigns that texture to scene.background and to scene.environment. Intensities are both 0.2. The commented-out ambient light is not in the render. If you drop the HDR on a phone, the materials are left with only the 0.014 directional light. Leave files, background, both intensities, and both rotations alone on every tier.

resolution on <Environment> does nothing in this file. Drei uses that prop only when Environment has children and renders them into a cube camera. This component passes files and no children, so the loader’s equirectangular HDR is what you see and what lights the meshes. Do not add resolution={128} and expect a smaller map.

What you do change:

  • Fill rate. The sky is a fullscreen sample of that HDR. The dpr cap in the table above is the mobile cut. Intensity stays 0.2, because a lower intensity changes the picture and does not shrink the texture.
  • Startup, only if the 1K file still stalls on a phone. Add a 512px .hdr of the same sky and choose that URL when isPhone is true. Same intensities, same rotations. That is a smaller download and a smaller upload. It is optional until you have measured a stall. Desktop keeps the 1K file.

Do not turn shadows on below lg

The fir and mushroom instanced meshes set castShadow and receiveShadow. The directional light does not set castShadow, and the canvas does not enable a shadow map, so those flags currently draw nothing. Leave it that way through md. A shadow map renders the fir cards and the mushrooms again from the light’s point of view, every frame. If you add shadows later, gate castShadow on the light to the desktop tier.

Build steps

  1. In the breakpoint hook, derive phone (≤ 767), compact (≤ 991), and desktop (≥ 992). Add a portrait query. Seed the first render from window.matchMedia so a phone does not mount the desktop scene first.
  2. In Experience, call the hook and pass the tier into the canvas, the orbit controls, and Terrain.
  3. Set 100dvh on html, body, #root, and the canvas. Set touch-action: none on the canvas.
  4. Apply the camera table. Keep the orbit target and the locked polar angle.
  5. Pass segments, treeCount, and mushroomCount from the quality table. Cap dpr.
  6. On the compact tier, omit bumpMap. On desktop, keep it.
  7. Leave the directional light’s color, intensity, and position unchanged. Leave the HDR URL, both intensities, and both rotations unchanged. Do not set resolution on this Environment. Do not enable shadow maps below lg.

Quiz

1. A phone held sideways at about 844px wide is:

md is 768–991. isXs || isSm stops at 767 and would leave this phone on the desktop scene.

2. useMediaQuery on the first render:

Seed the tier from window.matchMedia so the first allocation is the one you keep.

3. 100dvh belongs on:

On desktop the two viewport units match. On a phone, 100% parents still follow the large viewport, so the canvas cannot be the only one that changes.

4. Portrait needs a larger fov and a larger camera z because:

The desktop lens is 50° top to bottom. Portrait keeps that vertical coverage and loses the sides unless you open the lens and step back.

5. On a phone you should:

The directional light is cheap. The HDR is the sky and the image-based light. Ambient is commented out, so removing the HDR leaves almost no light.

6. resolution on the current <Environment files>:

Drei’s resolution belongs to the portal path. The sky’s per-frame cost drops when you cap dpr. A smaller .hdr file is a separate, optional download cut.
